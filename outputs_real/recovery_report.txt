  These two blocks answer different questions and must never be blended.
  A high R^2 in block 1 does not raise any number in block 2.

[ WHAT THE R^2 IS NOT ]
  - Recovering a planted constant is evidence about the estimator, not the biology. An R^2 of 0.99 here means the pipeline inverts a PDE it was handed; it is not predictive accuracy on cancer, and presenting it as an accuracy figure invites a question the benchmark cannot answer.
  - The planted transport field is a smooth analytic solution on an infinite domain sampled away from the boundary. Real tissue has heterogeneous diffusivity, binding, cellular uptake and convection, none of which the benchmark contains, so a small recovery error says nothing about transport accuracy in vivo.
  - The planted Cox cohort has exponential survival, proportional hazards and no unmeasured confounding. Real progression data has none of those properties, so recovering the planted coefficients to 1% is not evidence that the same estimator recovers real coefficients.
  - A recovery benchmark cannot fail on a bug it does not exercise. It covers the transport operator and the hazard estimator; it does not cover the clonal dynamics, the switch rule, or the endpoint translation, each of which carries its own assumptions.
  - Success here does not raise the real-cohort numbers. The held-out discrimination on real patients stays whatever it is, and the two measurements must be reported separately rather than blended.
